| # | Finding | Severity | Triage |
|---|---------|----------|--------|
| 1 | `time.Second << (n - free)` overflows `int64` from 34 doublings, so the wait came out negative and was skipped: after about 39 failures, a few guesses in a row went through unthrottled | Critical | **Accepted.** Past 30 doublings the wait is the ceiling. A test walks every count up to 2³²−1 |
| 2 | A refused attempt counts against every counter. One address already in a wait can push any user's counter to the ceiling without hashing anything, and a request every few minutes keeps a user waiting for good | Critical | **Sent to epistemer.** §5.8.3 and §7 say a refused attempt is counted, so changing that loosens a written rule. The status log of 2026-11-15 recommends counting only attempts that are let through |
| 3 | Concurrent successes from one address could leave a wait behind on a counter with no failures, depending on the order they finished in | Major | **Accepted.** When a success takes back its count from a counter others touched, the wait shrinks to what the remaining count would have set. A test finishes five successes in three orders. Its second half, an attempt refused because a success's count is still in flight and left counted, follows from finding 2 |
| 4 | `RetryAfter` on the sixth attempt is two seconds, not one | Major | **Rejected.** The fifth failure starts the one-second wait. The sixth attempt is refused and, per §5.8.3, counted, which doubles it. This changes if finding 2 does |
| 5 | `succeed` ran before the disabled check, which made a disabled user's password an oracle and cleared their counters | Major | **Accepted in part.** No oracle: `ErrUserDisabled` already answers a right password (§5.1). Counters are now cleared only by an accepted proof, the strict side |
| 6 | A success that finishes after its counter started again can take a new failure off it | Major | **Accepted.** A success older than `throttle.window` takes nothing off |
| 7 | A success from a registered device didn't clear the user's counter, which §5.8.5 says it does | Major | **Accepted.** A success clears the user's and the pair's counters whether or not the attempt was counted against them. The end-to-end device test waits for M2.3 |
| 8 | A storage error after counting leaves the attempt counted | Minor | **Rejected.** That is the strict side, and the review read a directory call that doesn't exist: the hash comes from auth's own store |
| 9 | Successes leave empty counters behind | Minor | **Accepted in part.** A counter left with no failure and no wait is deleted. `Sweep` (M1.5) removes the rest |
| 10 | Missing end-to-end tests: one login's wait at one address, a request with no address, a step-up | Minor | **Accepted.** Three tests |
