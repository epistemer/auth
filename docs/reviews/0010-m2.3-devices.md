# Review 0010: M2.3, devices

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | 2026-11-15 |
| Under review | The M2.3 diff, with interface §5.7, §5.8.6, §5.1.10, §7's device lines and amendment A12 |
| Reviewer | Gemini 4 Argon (High) through agy, about 84k tokens |
| Triage | Claude |

Nine findings: seven accepted, one in part, one mostly rejected. Every
accepted finding about behaviour has a test that fails without its fix.

| # | Finding | Severity | Triage |
|---|---------|----------|--------|
| 1 | A flow kept only the device's ID, so a token replaced since, by another `Finish`, still verified an older flow, gave it the throttle's exemption, and let it replace the token again | Critical | **Accepted.** A flow keeps the hash of the token it presented, and the device must still have it |
| 2 | A step-up that proved only a password registered a device, counting the second factor the session proved earlier | Critical | **Accepted.** The step-up must prove a second method itself |
| 3 | `Finish` replaced the device's token, and attached it to the session, though the sign-in hadn't used it: under `TrustDevices: false`, or after two factors | Major | **Accepted.** Only a sign-in that relied on the device replaces it; `RememberDevice` after two factors renews the device presented, with its new label |
| 4 | The throttle's exemption held for a device the policy doesn't trust, and before any proof; a stolen token could tell which login owns it | Major | **Accepted in part.** The exemption needs a device the policy trusts. §5.8.6 gives it before the proof by design: it is what keeps an owner's own device out of a stranger's wait |
| 5 | Setting a passwordless user's first password kept their devices | Major | **Accepted.** Any new password forgets them |
| 6 | Tests that would pass with their rule broken: the `Within` check, `MsgNewDevice` on reuse, `TrustDevices: false` at `Finish`, tokens after a password change | Major | **Accepted** |
| 7 | A device forgotten between the view and `Finish` failed a sign-in that the policy would allow unverified | Minor | **Accepted** |
| 8 | A session whose device was forgotten couldn't register a new one at a step-up | Minor | **Accepted** |
| 9 | Device lookups cost different work for known and unknown logins | Minor | **Mostly rejected.** The lookup now runs only when a token is presented; a stolen device token is a secret already, and what it costs to look one up says nothing more |

Mutation checks: each of 13 rules of the first pass and 7 of the fixes,
removed in turn, fails a test, but three equivalent: re-checking the user
in the first lookup (each later check repeats it), the in-transaction check
of a forgotten device, and its unverified fallback (both cover a race the
view before them already catches in sequence).
