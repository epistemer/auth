## The review

| # | Finding | Severity | Triage |
|---|---------|----------|--------|
| 1 | A `Challenge` racing the flow's first proof stored options made before it (no user, no credentials, so a passkey that needs `allowCredentials` then fails), or state for a method no longer offered; and past ten `Challenge`s, `Start` still ran before the cap refused | Medium | **Fixed.** `Challenge` refuses before asking the method once the caps are reached, and with `ErrMethodUnavailable` when the flow's proofs or user changed while `Start` ran, storing nothing; asked again, the method is told the user (`TestModuleChallengeRacesProof`) |
| 2 | No upper bound on an RSA modulus: a key of hundreds of thousands of bits fits in 64 KiB and makes every verification dear | Medium | **Fixed.** At most 4096 bits; no passkey uses more |
| 3 | The shop's passkey routes didn't check a flow's purpose, so an enrollment's answer posted to the sign-in route finished it without its recovery codes; a body past 64 KiB was cut and still used up the challenge; `/passkey/options` writes a flow for anyone | Medium | **Fixed in part.** `/passkey/prove` takes only a sign-in or a step-up, `/passkey/enroll/finish` only an enrollment; a body past 64 KiB is answered 413 first. Rate-limiting `/passkey/options` is the app's, as for any route anyone may call; its comment says so, and `Sweep` removes the flows |
| 4 | `passkey.New` panicked on a typed nil config | Low | **Fixed**, as `auth.New` does |

The reviewer found these hold up across the three milestones: a passkey
as both a first proof and a second factor, with UP and UV required;
cross-user sign-in, step-up and recovery; nothing told to a method before
a sessionless flow's first proof; one judgement per state and the
credential checked again in the write transaction; disabled users and
stale flows updating nothing; duplicates and the limit of 20 checked in
`Finish`'s transaction; address-only throttling of passkeys with trusted
devices; no secret in events or errors; and CSRF on the shop's routes.
